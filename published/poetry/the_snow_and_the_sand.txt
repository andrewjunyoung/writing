Driving to the coastline, I pull over to the right.
I park; pack a small light backpack; lock up; and set out.
Every step in the deep snow is new friction
as I walk along the frosted moraine towards the sea.
The friction of each step brings resistance, forcing me to exert harder;
to move on, with ever more joy, like a hound on its first walk after a long sickness
On the journey to the coastline, I battle with the snow pack
foot by foot into the ice
and i force myself back up
and forward.
I pass the sign for the summer beaches
and the old wood pier now obscured by its own frosting
and the frozen marsh
and the cottages
and the abandoned wier
And I climb the last ice bank.
Over the hill, I see the sea.
I smell the ocean.
I hear the croaks of gulls.
I fall to my knees and run my hads through the sand
and crisp snow,
for surely this is the reason that in our childhood years, we learn to touch and feel and to live and to search for peace
I feel the sand and the snow at the end of the world and I know I will one day make it again out here, to such peace.
